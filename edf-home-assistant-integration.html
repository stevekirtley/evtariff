Why this exists

EDF runs on Kraken. Home Assistant didn't know.

EDF Energy moved its customers onto Kraken, the same platform Octopus Energy runs on. That gave EDF a proper API, but the well-known Home Assistant integration for Kraken is built for Octopus and won't talk to EDF's endpoint. EDF customers were left with nothing that understood their tariff, their Go Electric smart charging, or the Flextras free electricity schemes.

HomeAssistant-EDFEnergy fixes that. It started as an adaptation of BottlecapDave's Octopus integration, so the entity names, rate handling and Energy Dashboard statistics follow a shape thousands of Home Assistant users already know. Since then it has grown EDF-specific features that don't exist anywhere else.

What you get

Everything on your EDF account, as Home Assistant entities

One login, and the integration creates devices for your electricity and gas meters, your account, and any EV charger enrolled in Smart Charging.

💷

Rates and standing charges

Current, previous and next unit rates for electricity and gas, half hour by half hour, plus standing charges. Time-of-use windows are read from the tariff itself, so Go Electric's 23:00 to 06:00 off-peak just works. If EDF hide your product from their pricing API, the rates come from your account agreement instead.

📊

Consumption and cost statistics

Half-hourly consumption and cost from your smart meter, imported as long-term statistics for the Energy Dashboard. Daily, weekly and monthly totals, with peak and off-peak splits on multi-rate tariffs, and cost trackers for any energy entity you like.

🚗

Go Electric Smart Charging

A "Smart Charging Dispatching" sensor that turns on during allocated smart slots, with planned and started dispatches as attributes. Switches for smart charge and bump charge, and controls for charge target and ready-by time. Rates during dispatches are adjusted to off-peak automatically.

Andersen, Ohme, Hypervolt, myenergi and more
⚡

Flextras and Power Perks free electricity

EDF announce Power Perks sessions by text message and nowhere else. The integration picks them up anyway and publishes every free electricity window as a calendar, next-session sensors and events, so your battery, EV and heavy loads can run on free power without you lifting a finger.

🖥️

The EDF Energy panel

A sidebar panel that shows your Flextras status, free electricity history, and the schemes your tariff is eligible for, with buttons to join or opt out. No YAML required.

🔍

Tariff comparison

Compare what yesterday would have cost on any other EDF tariff, using your real consumption. Handy for deciding whether Go Electric, a fixed deal or the standard variable rate is right for you.

Things people build with it

Every rate, dispatch and free session is a normal Home Assistant entity, so it slots straight into automations, dashboards and the Energy panel.

  • Force-charge a home battery through the Go Electric off-peak window, and hold it back during the evening peak.
  • Start the dishwasher, tumble dryer or immersion heater the moment a Power Perks free electricity session begins, and stop them when it ends.
  • Pause a battery export when a Smart Charging slot is allocated, so the car gets the cheap power first.
  • Send a phone notification with tomorrow's free electricity window as soon as EDF announce it.
  • Track exactly what an EV, heat pump or hot tub costs per day on your real tariff, not a guessed flat rate.
  • Show today's rate curve on a dashboard card, with dispatches and free sessions marked.
Install

Up and running in a few minutes

The integration is installed through HACS, the Home Assistant Community Store. It's currently awaiting review for the HACS default catalogue, so for now you add it as a custom repository. The button below does that for you.

  1. Add the repository to HACS. Click Add to HACS, or in HACS go to Integrations → ⋮ → Custom repositories and paste https://github.com/stevekirtley/HomeAssistant-EDFEnergy with the category Integration.
  2. Download it from HACS, then restart Home Assistant.
  3. Add the integration. Go to Settings → Devices & services → Add integration and search for EDF Energy.
  4. Sign in with your EDF email address and password, plus your account number, which looks like A-AAAA1111 and is at the top of any bill. Your password is used once to create an API key and is never stored.

FAQ

Common questions

Is this an official EDF Energy integration?

No. EDF don't publish one. This is an independent, open-source project that talks to the same Kraken API the EDF app uses. It isn't affiliated with EDF Energy or Kraken.

Is it in HACS?

It has been submitted to the HACS default catalogue and is in the review queue. Until it appears in HACS search, add it as a custom repository using the button above. Updates arrive through HACS either way.

Which EDF tariffs does it support?

All of them. Fixed, standard variable, Economy 7, and the Go Electric EV tariffs. Smart Charging entities only appear if your account has an EV charger enrolled in EDF Smart Charging.

Do I need a smart meter?

Rates, standing charges, Smart Charging and free electricity sessions work without one. Half-hourly consumption and cost statistics need a smart meter sending half-hourly readings to EDF, which is also what Go Electric requires.

I'm on Octopus, not EDF. Can I use it?

No. Octopus customers should use BottlecapDave's Octopus Energy integration, which this project was adapted from. If you're weighing up a move to EDF, the guides on this site cover the numbers.

How do Power Perks sessions get into Home Assistant?

EDF only announce them by text message. A small relay reads the text from the phone holding the registered number, works out the window, and publishes it. Every install reads that feed, so nothing needs configuring. There's also an action to enter a session by hand.

Is it free?

Yes. MIT licensed, no accounts, no telemetry. If it saves you money, there's a GitHub Sponsors page, and if you switch to EDF you're welcome to use the referral link.
