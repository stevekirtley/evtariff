Why I switched

So why did I leave Intelligent Octopus Go?

I was a loyal Octopus customer. Looking back, I don't really know why. Loyalty should work both ways, and Octopus weren't very loyal to me.

Long-term Intelligent Go customers didn't seem to be getting a fair deal on pricing. Then in May 2026 Octopus put the price up again and started enforcing a charge cap that's far more complicated than it needs to be. That was enough.

Go Electric gave me a lower off-peak rate, a longer window and no cap, and it didn't care what car or charger I had.

For context: I drive an MG5, charge on an Andersen Quartz, and do about 10,000 miles a year.

12 months or 18? The 12-month has the lower day rate in every region. The 18-month has the lower standing charge, by about 5p a day, and fixes your prices for six months longer, with a £100 exit fee instead of £75. If you use a lot of electricity in the day, the 12-month wins on price today.

I started on the 12-month. But with the way the market's going and where prices are predicted to head, I'd rather have my rates fixed for longer, so I moved to the 18-month.

💡 Changing your mind mid-contract? Ask EDF. I asked nicely to move from the 12-month to the 18-month Go Electric, and they very kindly waived my exit fee. There's no guarantee they'll do the same for you, but it costs nothing to ask.

My real numbers

What I've actually used

Rates are one thing. Here's what I've actually used, straight from EDF's data through my Home Assistant integration.

The car

My MG5 does about 10,000 miles a year and averages around 3.3 miles per kWh. That's roughly 3,030 kWh a year, which at 6.66p comes to about £202 a year, or 2p a mile.

A petrol car doing 45mpg would need about 1,010 litres for the same miles. At the UK average of 167p a litre in mid-November 2026, that's around £1,690. Charging overnight on Go Electric saves me roughly £1,490 a year on fuel.

The whole house

My house isn't typical. Alongside the car I've got solar panels and a GivEnergy battery. The battery doesn't change how much electricity I use, it changes when I buy it: it fills up overnight at 6.66p and runs the house through the day. So almost none of what I import is at the peak rate.

Switching

What the switch was actually like

You can't go straight onto Go Electric. You join EDF on its standard variable tariff, and once EDF can read half-hourly data from your smart meter, you move to Go Electric in your account or the app.

The one hiccup came before any of that. I have an export meter for my solar, and EDF's online sign-up is a bit clunky when a house has an export meter as well as an import one, so I had to phone to start my switch. After that it was easy: three days on the standard variable tariff, then straight onto the cheap overnight rate (6.99p back then, before the VAT cut).

💡 Got solar export? If the online sign-up won't let you through, call EDF on 0333 006 9950. If you're using a referral link, give it to them on that first call. EDF won't add a referral afterwards if it wasn't mentioned then.

Smart Charging

Smart Charging: does the £5 a month turn up?

Smart Charging is optional. If your car or charger is supported, EDF schedules your charging and pays £5 a month in bill credit. There's no six-hour cap like Octopus has. If your kit isn't supported, you just set a timer for after 23:00 and get the same 6.66p rate.

I use it with my MG5 and Andersen Quartz, and it's been reliable. The £5 credit has arrived every month, and it's always hit my charge target, as long as that was possible in the time I gave it.

I don't use EDF's app to set it up. I set my charge targets through Home Assistant, using my EDF integration.

The one thing that nearly kept me on Octopus was Home Assistant. There were a few EDF integrations around, but nothing fully featured. So I built one: HomeAssistant-EDFEnergy is a fork of BottleCapDave's Octopus integration, adapted for EDF's API, and it's MIT licensed.

Home Assistant has taken the place of the EDF app for me. The app isn't great, so I built everything I wanted from it into the integration: I check my usage, see my bills and control my Smart Charging from there.

Should you switch?

Who Go Electric suits, and who it doesn't

Switch if your car or charger isn't on Octopus's or E.ON's compatibility list, you're on standard Octopus Go, you need more than six hours of cheap charging, or you can move a lot of household use into the 23:00–06:00 window.

Think twice if you use a lot of electricity during the day, might move house within the fixed term, or rely on Octopus extras like Saving Sessions.

FAQ

EDF Go Electric questions

Does EDF Go Electric work with any EV and charger?

Yes. You set a timer on your car or charger to start after 23:00 and get 6.66p/kWh until 06:00. Smart Charging is the optional extra that needs a supported car or charger.

Is the off-peak rate whole-home?

Yes. Everything your house uses between 23:00 and 06:00 is charged at 6.66p/kWh, not just the car.

Is there a cap on smart charging like Octopus?

No. Octopus limits smart-scheduled cheap charging to six hours a day. EDF's Smart Charging has no equivalent cap.

How do I move my export to EDF and get the 13p rate?

Move your import first. EDF's 13p fixed export rate is only for customers who also buy their electricity from EDF, so apply for export once your import switch has gone through. Allow a few weeks for it to complete. If the online form won't accept your application, phone EDF. I had to phone to switch anyway because of my export meter.

How long until I'm on Go Electric?

Usually two or three days. You join on the standard variable tariff, and EDF moves you over once it can read half-hourly data from your smart meter. I was on standard for three days. If it's been more than a week, phone EDF.

Which free-electricity schemes do I get on Go Electric?

Power Perks, plus 4 bonus hours when you join Flextras. You book the bonus hours in hourly slots, and they last a year. Weekend Saver isn't available on time-of-use tariffs like Go Electric.

My Power Perks credit hasn't arrived. Is that normal?

Yes. EDF's Power Perks terms say credits are normally issued during the first week of the month after the event. Depending on your billing date, it can be a bill or two before you see the credit. If it still hasn't appeared after that, or doesn't match what you used, chase EDF.

Does Smart Charging give my whole house the cheap rate?

Yes. Any slot EDF schedules outside 23:00–06:00 is charged at the off-peak rate for everything your house uses, not just the car. The app can show those slots at the peak rate at first, but your bill gets it right.

Should I link my car or my charger for Smart Charging?

The charger, if you can. A charger talks to EDF directly, while car integration also relies on the car maker's servers, so there's more to go wrong. Some cars, including BMW and Mini, start charging as soon as you plug in when they're linked through the car. Set a charging schedule in the car and let EDF adjust it.

How do I use a Power Perks session without losing Smart Charging?

Don't switch Smart Charging off, or you may not get your £5 credit that month. If you need to plug in early, set your charge to the minimum (10%) and your ready-by time to the latest (11am). Then press boost when the free window starts.

Do I need an EV for Go Electric?

Yes. EDF's terms require you to have an EV. Some customers say EDF told them otherwise on calls or in emails, but the terms are what EDF might hold you to. In the worst case, EDF could move you to the standard variable tariff, or bill you at its rates.

12-month or 18-month?

If most of your electricity goes through overnight, take the 18-month. You get your rates fixed through two winters, and the standing charge is lower. I started on the 12-month and asked EDF nicely if I could move to the 18-month, and they waived my exit fee. There's no guarantee they'll do the same for you, but there's no harm in asking.

EDF set my Direct Debit far too high. What do I do?

Ask EDF to switch you to a variable Direct Debit, so you pay what you actually used each month. It's the easiest fix, especially if you've got solar or a battery.

If EDF brings out a new version before my switch, which rate do I get?

The rate you booked. Your price is locked when you book the switch, not when it completes.

Why isn't my usage showing in the app?

Give it two or three days after your switch. Even after that, the EDF app runs about a day behind. If you want something better, use Home Assistant with my integration, or EnergyDock.

Should I move my gas to EDF too?

Only if it's the cheapest option for you. Gas is a separate supply, so you can keep it anywhere. I moved mine to Fuse because it had a lower standing charge and a longer fix. Here's why I chose Fuse for gas.
